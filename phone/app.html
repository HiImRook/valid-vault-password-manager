Personal Info

Your name, phone, emails, and address, for filling in forms. Viewing requires unlock; adding or editing any field asks for your fingerprint or device PIN, with your master password as the fallback. Social Security numbers and card info are held back for later, added security work.

Misc Credentials

Store a secret under a category, like a Wi-Fi password, license key, or security code.

Crypto Wallets

Store seed phrases by wallet and account. Words stay hidden until you tap Show, and hide again when you switch tabs, leave the app, or the timer runs out.

Bookmarks

Encrypted bookmarks, the same list as your browser extension once you sync. Tap a bookmark to open it in your browser. To save a page from any app, tap Share and choose Valid Vault.

User Guide

How Valid Vault works

Everything is encrypted on this phone under one master key. Nothing goes to a server, and there is no account. Your fingerprint, device PIN, or master password each unlock that same key.

Each section (logins, misc credentials, personal info, crypto wallets) asks you to unlock again before it shows anything.

Autofill in other apps and browsers

Turn it on in Settings, Autofill, Turn on, then choose Valid Vault. In Chrome, also turn on Settings, Passwords, Autofill using another service.

Tap a login field and choose Valid Vault from the suggestions. Confirm with your fingerprint, device PIN, or password. With one saved login for the site it fills right away; with several, pick the one you want. Only that login is handed to Android.

In browsers, Valid Vault matches the website the same way the extension does. Apps only tell Android their name, so the first time you fill in an app you pick which saved site it belongs to. Settings, Linked apps lists these links and lets you remove one.

When you sign in somewhere new, Android offers to save the login to Valid Vault. Confirm with your fingerprint and it is encrypted like any other login.

To save a page from any app as a bookmark, tap Share and choose Valid Vault.

Valid Vault has two home screen widgets. The small one is a Search privately bar with a Bookmarks button. The larger one adds Bookmarks, Logins, Wallet, and Personal buttons. Each button opens Valid Vault, asks for your fingerprint or device PIN once, and goes straight to that section.

Private browser

Valid Vault includes a Chromium browser for private browsing. Open it from Settings, from a Valid Vault widget, or by long-pressing the app icon and tapping Search. Tapping a bookmark opens it there too.

The bar along the bottom has Back, Bookmarks, New tab, Share, and Forward. Both bars slide away as you scroll down a page and come back when you scroll up. Pull down from the top of a page to reload it. Bookmarks opens Valid Vault and asks for your fingerprint or device PIN. The menu at the top right has Add to new tab quick view, the tracker switch for the site, Tor mode, Valid Vault settings, and About. In Tor mode, New identity, Safer, and Fill login sit at the top of it instead.

Up to 15 tabs can be open at once. The number beside the address bar shows how many are open. Tap it to see your tabs, switch between them, close one, or close them all. Press and hold it to open a new tab. Press and hold a link to open it in a new tab, copy it, or share it. Tabs you aren't looking at are paused.

Each new tab opens on the start page, with its own background. Add sites with Add to new tab quick view in the menu, or with the Add site tile, which asks for a name and address. Up to 12 fit. New sites go at the bottom and earlier rows move up. Press and hold a site and drag it onto another to reorder them, or press, hold and let go to edit its name and address, open it in a new tab, or remove it. Tap the address bar to search or type an address.

Like a background? Tap Save it to Downloads under it to keep a copy of that exact picture, for a wallpaper or to share.

Type a web address to go straight to it, or anything else to search. Startpage is the default search engine. DuckDuckGo and Brave Search are in Settings, Browser.

Known trackers and ad scripts are blocked, using Valid Vault's own list and the EasyPrivacy and EasyList lists, and the empty space a blocked ad leaves is removed. Tracking tags such as utm and fbclid, and share tags like YouTube's si, are removed from links, and redirect wrappers from Google, Facebook, and others are skipped so you land on the real page. Third-party cookies are off, and every site is sent Global Privacy Control, which asks it not to sell or share your data.

The start page shows how many trackers were blocked this session. If a site breaks, use the tracker switch in the menu to turn blocking off for that site. Settings, Browser lists those sites so you can turn blocking back on.

When a page offers a file, the browser shows its name and size and asks before downloading it to your Downloads folder.

Login fields get a small green V. Tap it and Valid Vault asks for your fingerprint or password, then fills your saved login for that site. Every fill asks again. If the site has no saved login but another part of it does, such as login.example.com when example.com is saved, those logins are offered first. When you sign in with a login Valid Vault doesn't have yet, or with a new password, the browser asks whether to save it. Pages that aren't using https ask before filling.

Name, email, phone, and address fields get a V too. Tap one and, after your fingerprint, Valid Vault lists what it can fill on that form from Personal Info, with a choice of your saved emails. Uncheck anything you don't want and tap Fill. Fields you already typed in are left alone. On a login form, an email field with no saved login offers your saved emails instead.

New password boxes on sign-up and change password forms get an inverted V, a green tag with a dark V. Tap it and pick a style, with special characters or letters and numbers only, and a strong password fills the new and confirm boxes, using the lengths set in Settings and any rules the site gives. The browser offers to save it when you submit.

Closing the browser wipes cookies, site data, cache, and history. It runs separately from your vault and only receives the one login you choose to fill, for the page you're on.

Tor mode sends every page through Orbot, the Tor Project's app, so sites can't see your IP address or location. Install Orbot and tap Start, then choose Turn Tor mode on from the browser's menu. The bars turn purple while it is on. If Orbot stops, pages stop loading until it is running again. Turning Tor mode on or off closes every tab and wipes the session, and each time the browser opens it starts in normal mode.

In Tor mode, downloads are off and New identity in the menu closes every tab and clears the session. Safer turns JavaScript off for extra protection, though some sites lose menus, logins, or video while it is on.

Signing in to a site tells that site who you are. When you're done with a site you signed into, tap New identity and refresh the connection in Orbot before you visit anything else.

In Tor mode, logins never fill on their own. The green V is off and the browser never offers to save, so use Fill login in the menu. It only works on https pages, needs Safer off, and reminds you once each session that signing in tells the site who you are. Personal Info and the password generator are off in Tor mode.

For a VPN, or Orbot in VPN mode, the browser's traffic goes through it like any other app.

Moving to or from another device

Both devices need the same master key before vaults can merge. Do it in this order:

1. On the device that already has your vault, use Share Master Key, or Export Key to make a key file.

2. On the new device, scan that key or open the file, then enter the passphrase and three answers.

3. Then move the vault: Share Vault and scan it, or Export Vault and import the file. Vault files carry everything, including bookmarks and crypto wallets. The vault QR carries logins, misc credentials, personal info, and bookmarks. Crypto wallets travel only in vault files.

Importing a vault merges it. The newest edit of each item wins, deleted items stay deleted, and the same login never shows up twice. Importing the same file again changes nothing.

Backups and USB drives

Export Vault saves an encrypted file to Downloads, or sends it with any app you choose. Copy it to a USB drive or another computer for a cold backup. It is useless without your master key.

Export Key saves your master key wrapped under your passphrase and three answers. Keep it separate from the vault file if you can.

Importing from another app

Sync, Import From Another App brings in logins from another password manager and bookmarks from a browser. The file is read on this phone and encrypted straight into your vault. Valid Vault never exports passwords unencrypted.

For passwords, export your logins from your old password manager, tap Import Passwords, choose the file, and confirm with your fingerprint or device PIN. Valid Vault reads CSV exports from most password managers and browsers, Samsung Pass .spass files, Bitwarden JSON files, 1Password .1pux files, and Dashlane's zipped export. For a protected file, like a Samsung Pass file, enter the password you set when you exported it. It is used once to open the file and is never saved. For any other CSV, you pick which columns hold the website, username, and password. Check the preview, then tap Import.

Logins saved for apps, like those in Google Password Manager and Samsung Pass, are matched to a website. When the app's site is in the file or your vault with the same username and password, it is matched for you. Otherwise you confirm a suggested site or skip that app. Matched apps are linked, so their logins fill in the app too.

When the file has a different password for a login you already have, you see both, masked, with when each was last changed and when yours was last used. Pick the one that is current. The other is kept as the previous password for 30 days, so you can switch back if you picked wrong. If the file has the same login twice with different passwords, you pick which one to import.

Stop import leaves your vault exactly as it was. Logins you deleted stay deleted, unless the file shows the password was changed after you deleted it.

When the import is done, delete the export file. A CSV, JSON, or zip export holds your passwords unencrypted, a protected file is only as safe as its export password, and Valid Vault can't delete it for you.

For bookmarks, export them from your browser as an HTML file and tap Import Bookmarks. Folders are combined into one list, links you already have are skipped, and bookmarks you deleted stay deleted.

Unlocking and auto-lock

Fingerprint or device PIN is the quickest way in. The secret stays in the phone's secure hardware. A master password works as a backup.

If you add a new fingerprint to your phone, Android resets Valid Vault's fingerprint unlock for safety. Unlock with your password, then re-enroll in Manage.

The vault locks after the idle time in Settings, or when you tap Lock.

What can and cannot be recovered

Nothing is lost while you can still unlock on any device that has your vault. A forgotten master password can be replaced from Manage while you are unlocked.

If this phone will not unlock, for example after Android resets fingerprint unlock and no password is set, tap Can't unlock? on the lock screen and use your key file or a key QR from your other device.

A lost key-file passphrase or answers cannot be recovered by anyone. Write them down and store them safely.

Scanning safely

A shared key QR is protected by your passphrase and answers. A shared vault QR is encrypted under your master key. Still, scan somewhere private.

About Valid Vault

Valid Vault Password Manager

Hi, I'm Rook.

I made a password manager. I found myself wanting two things I just wasn't getting: relevant features and far less betrayal.

This is my best effort at common sense features with low friction, and how about we try zero betrayal?

Valid Vault is fully local. The only person who sees, or even touches, your data is you. There is no cloud sync, by design. To me, the idea of this data, YOUR data, traveling across countries and even continents and then back to you is insane. That data never needed to leave your device. More things should be handled locally, and cloud connectivity should be approached with caution. Unfortunately, we see the opposite.
